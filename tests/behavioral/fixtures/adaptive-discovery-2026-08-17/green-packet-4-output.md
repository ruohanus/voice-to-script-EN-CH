For six Saturdays, I promised my daughter we'd make pancakes.

And six times, I answered a work message before breakfast was finished.

I was on customer-support rotation.

The messages were real.

Another teammate was on rotation too.

I answered because I was fast at it.

I liked being the reliable one.

One Saturday, she put a pancake on a plate and asked,

"Should I save you one?"

I hadn't missed the whole morning.

I came back eighteen minutes later.

That was almost worse.

Eighteen minutes was small enough to excuse.

I could still tell myself I'd been responsible.

I wrote this in my notes afterward:

I had been calling every interruption responsible.

The next Saturday, I left my phone in the hallway until breakfast ended.

The pancakes were uneven.

She told me a long story about a school project.

My phone buzzed twice.

Nothing collapsed.

I'd also been protecting my place as the fast, reliable one.

Answering fastest isn't always the same as caring most.
