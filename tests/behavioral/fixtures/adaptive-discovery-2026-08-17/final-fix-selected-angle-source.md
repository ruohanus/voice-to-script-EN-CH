I used to answer every small question on my team because being useful made me feel secure. One Tuesday, a designer moved a deadline from Tuesday to Wednesday while I was on a client call because she assumed she still needed my approval. The client waited, the designer waited, and I came out of the call feeling important for about ten seconds. Then I realized I had built a team that paused when I disappeared.

I kept telling everyone I wanted ownership from them, but I corrected small decisions before they could learn from them. A colleague had told me that I was moving too quickly for anyone else to take the wheel. I did not believe her at the time. After the delayed deadline, I chose two kinds of decisions the team could make without me for a three-week experiment. I am relieved, but I still do not know whether I will trust the system when a decision goes badly.

I can imagine this being about control, the identity of being useful, or what delegation actually costs.
