For nearly a year, I kept telling people I was "working on the handbook" for our team, but most weeks I did anything except write it. I would schedule a morning to work on it, then see a message, say yes to a request, move the block, and call that flexibility. I wrote this explanation down three times because I thought the explanation itself would prove I understood the problem: I was busy, so the handbook could wait. I was useful in the moment, so the handbook could wait. I was responding to urgent things, so the handbook could wait. By Friday, the handbook could still wait. The old way of saying it is that I was prioritizing the team, but that is not quite true because the handbook was for the team too.

The clearest day was a Wednesday. I opened the draft at 9:00. By 9:12 I had answered a request about a customer question. By 9:35 I had joined a call that did not need me. At 10:20 I came back and could not remember why the second section mattered. I spent the rest of the hour rereading the first section, then declared it was not a good writing day. That sounds minor, except it happened often enough that new people kept asking the same basic questions, and the people who already knew the answers kept becoming the support system for everyone else.

I finally tried something embarrassingly small: two afternoons with notifications off and a single place for questions. The first afternoon felt selfish. I kept thinking I should make myself available before anyone asked. I did not become serene or efficient. I just stayed with the awkwardness long enough to finish the section on how we make decisions. A week later, someone used it without asking me for help.

I do not think messages are the enemy and I do not think everyone can protect long blocks of time. I do think I had confused being easy to reach with making things easier for other people. I want to end with a practical invitation, not a productivity slogan.
