## English

For months, I kept my camera off in meetings,  
even when I had prepared something useful to say.

I would sit there with the thought ready,  
then decide it needed one more check.

That habit started after one early meeting.

I explained an idea too quickly,  
and someone asked a question I couldn't answer.

Nobody laughed.  
Nobody blamed me.

But I treated that moment like a warning.

I started believing I should only speak  
when I was certain.

So I found another way to contribute.

After meetings, I sent careful follow-up notes.

By then, I could choose every word.  
I could make the idea complete.

The notes may have been useful,  
but I was entering the conversation after it had already happened.

I wasn't helping people see the confusion  
while they were still inside it.

Then a project stalled.

Two people were making different assumptions  
about the same deadline.

I noticed the mismatch during the meeting.

My first instinct was familiar:  
write a detailed note afterward.

I could explain the difference clearly,  
once I had checked everything.

Halfway through the call,  
I said that I thought we were solving two different problems.

Even then, I almost wanted a full answer  
before I let myself say anything.

But I didn't have one.

I only knew the conversation didn't line up.

Speaking up doesn't always change a meeting.

Uncertainty isn't useful on its own.

Sometimes uncertainty is just uncertainty.

I still wait too long sometimes.

I still know how easy it is  
to promise myself I'll write the perfect note later.

The smaller rule I'm practicing is this:

If I can name the confusion accurately,  
I don't have to solve the whole problem before I speak.

So I asked each person which date they were using,  
and why.

Once the difference was visible,  
the group agreed on a next step.

After the meeting,  
I worried that I had sounded obvious rather than helpful.

A teammate later told me  
the question had saved them a week of work.

I keep coming back to that.

What felt too obvious to say  
was exactly what the group needed to see.

When I can name the confusion accurately,  
I can enter the conversation before I have the solution.

That changes what I count as useful.

I used to think a useful contribution  
had to arrive as an answer.

Now I think it can also be  
the question that makes the real problem visible.

I don't know whether I'll keep doing this  
when the stakes feel higher.

That part is still open.

Recommended ending — Story request — best for comments

I still don't know if I'll do this when the stakes are higher.  
When did naming the confusion help before you had the answer?

Alternate endings

Named reader — best for shares

To the teammate who told me that uncertain question saved a week:  
I still don't know if I'll speak when the stakes are higher.

Callback — best for cohesion

I kept my camera off because I thought certainty had to come first.  
Now I name confusion, but higher stakes may still silence me.

Unapologized position

I still don't know if I'll speak when the stakes are higher.  
Until I find out, naming the confusion is enough reason to speak.

Light delivery notes

Start quietly and leave a full pause after “Nobody blamed me.”  
Slow down on the smaller rule, then let the final question stay open.

## 简体中文

有好几个月，  
我开会时一直关着摄像头。

哪怕我提前准备过，  
也确实有一些有用的话想说，  
我还是很少在当下开口。

这个习惯，  
是从一次很早的会议开始的。

那次我把一个想法讲得太快，  
有人问了一个我答不上来的问题。

没有人笑我，  
也没有人怪我。

可我把那一刻当成了一个警告。

我开始觉得，  
只有确定的时候，我才应该说话。

于是，我换了一种参与方式。

会后，  
我会发很仔细的补充说明。

我可以慢慢整理，  
把每一个词都检查一遍，  
等想法完整了再发出去。

这些说明也许有用。

可等我写完，  
真正的讨论已经结束了。

我是在会后进入对话，  
而不是在大家还没想明白的时候，  
帮忙把困惑说出来。

后来，有个项目卡住了。

两个人对同一个截止日期，  
其实用了两套不同的假设。

我在会上听出了不对劲。

我的第一个反应还是老样子：  
先别说，等会后写清楚。

我想等自己把差异确认完整，  
再发一条足够周全的说明。

但那次，会议开到一半，  
我还是说了出来。

我只是说，  
我觉得大家可能在解决两个不同的问题。

我没有完整答案。

我只知道，  
眼前这段对话没有对上。

开口不会每次都改变一场会议。

不确定本身也不一定有用。

有时候，  
不确定就只是不确定。

而且我现在还是会等太久。

我还是很容易告诉自己，  
会后再写一份更完整的说明就好。

我现在只练一条很小的规则：

只要我能准确说出困惑在哪里，  
就不用先把整个问题解决了再开口。

所以那次，我问了两个人，  
他们各自用的是哪个日期，  
为什么会用那个日期。

差异一摆出来，  
大家就同意了下一步怎么走。

可会后，我又开始担心。

我会不会只是说了一个太明显的问题，  
听起来根本没有帮上忙？

后来，一位队友告诉我，  
那个问题替他们省下了一周的工作。

这句话我一直记着。

当时我觉得太明显、不值得说的东西，  
正是大家需要一起看见的东西。

如果我能把混乱说准确，  
就可以先进入对话，  
不用等有了完整答案才开口。

这也改变了我对有用的理解。

以前我觉得，  
有用的发言应该带着答案出现。

现在我慢慢觉得，  
有用的发言也可以只是一个问题，  
先让真正的问题被看见。

我不知道，  
等事情更重要、压力更大的时候，  
我还能不能继续这样做。

这件事还没有答案。

Recommended ending — Story request — best for comments

事情更重要时，我能不能继续这样做，我还是不知道。  
你有没有一次，是在没有答案时先说出了困惑？

Alternate endings

Named reader — best for shares

写给那位告诉我，这个问题省下一周工作的队友：  
事情更重要时我还能不能开口，我还是不知道。

Callback — best for cohesion

我曾关着摄像头，等自己完全确定才开口。  
现在我会先说困惑，但压力更大时能否做到，我不知道。

Unapologized position

事情更重要时我还能不能开口，我还是不知道。  
在知道答案之前，我还是会先把困惑说出来。

Light delivery notes

开头放轻，讲到“没有人怪我”后停一下。  
说那条小规则时放慢，最后的问题不要急着收住。
