The seed swap wasn't a disaster.

We held it.  
People came.  
Seeds changed hands.

I know that sounds like I'm lowering the bar.

But for the three weeks before it,  
I answered almost every question myself.

I approved every table label.  
I checked every change to the signup sheet.

I checked once.  
Then again.  
Then once more.

I did care about getting it right.

But the more uncomfortable admission is that  
I liked being the person who knew everything.

Which table had the herbs.  
Who had promised envelopes.  
Whether we had enough jars.

Every question made me feel necessary.  
That's hard to admit.

So I kept replying before anyone else could.

There were six other volunteers in the planning chat.  
Two of them had run community events before.

I still rewrote the shared instructions on Thursday night.

Then I messaged everyone Friday morning  
to explain the rewrite.

Then I answered questions about the explanation.

Saturday gave me the clearest version of the problem.

We opened at ten.

At 9:40, one volunteer moved the tomato seeds  
to two tables.

The line had been blocking the door.

I almost moved them back,  
because that wasn't the layout I had approved.

I didn't move them.

The line shortened.  
People found the tables.  
Nothing dramatic happened.

Twenty minutes later, someone asked another volunteer  
where the empty envelopes were.

That volunteer answered without looking for me.

I felt relieved.  
And then I felt strangely left out.

Feeling unnecessary bothered me.  
That's the embarrassing part.

I'd said I wanted a shared event.

But I'd built a small system where sharing meant  
helping me carry out my answers.

I kept calling it care because control sounded uglier.  
The next week, we changed three things.

One shared page held the current plan.

Questions went to the group  
instead of straight to me.

And each table had one person  
who could change its setup on the day.

I didn't become relaxed about it.

I checked the page once.  
Then again.

I stopped before once more.

One volunteer changed the herb labels.  
I would have used different words.

Her labels worked.

Another volunteer noticed we had too many jars.  
She moved half of them to the free table.

I hadn't planned that.  
People took them.

I could turn this into a clean lesson about trust,  
but that would let me hide inside another answer.

What changed was smaller.

The tomato tables moved.  
The line shortened.  
And I didn't move them back.
