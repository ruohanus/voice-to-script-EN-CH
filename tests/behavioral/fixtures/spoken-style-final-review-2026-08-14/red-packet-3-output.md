## English

How certain do I need to be before I speak?

For months, my answer was: completely certain.

So I kept my camera off in meetings,  
even when I'd prepared something useful to say.

It started with one early meeting.

I explained an idea too quickly,  
and someone asked a question I couldn't answer.

Nobody laughed.

Nobody blamed me.

But I treated that moment as a warning.

I decided I should only speak when I was certain.

After that, I changed how I participated.

Instead of entering the conversation while it was happening,  
I sent careful follow-up notes after meetings.

The notes gave me time to make every sentence precise.

They also kept me out of the uncertain part,  
when people were still asking questions and changing direction.

I could tell myself I was still contributing.

I had prepared.

I had noticed something useful.

I had written it down.

The only difference was timing.

Then a project stalled.

Two people were making different assumptions  
about the same deadline.

I noticed the mismatch during a meeting.

My first plan was familiar.

Stay quiet.

Write a detailed note afterward.

Make sure I could explain the whole thing.

Halfway through the call, I didn't do that.

I said, "I think we're solving two different problems."

That was all I knew for certain.

I didn't have the full answer.

So I asked each person what date they were using,  
and why they were using it.

Once they said the dates out loud,  
the difference was visible to everyone.

The group agreed on a next step.

Afterward, I started reviewing what I'd said.

I worried that I'd sounded obvious rather than helpful.

Maybe everyone else had already seen the mismatch.

Maybe I'd only pointed at something they already knew.

I hadn't solved the whole problem.

I'd only named the confusion accurately.

Later, a teammate told me that question had saved them a week of work.

I don't take that as proof  
that speaking up always changes a meeting.

And I don't think uncertainty is useful by itself.

Sometimes a half-formed thought is just a half-formed thought.

I still wait too long sometimes.

But I'm trying to practice a smaller rule now.

If I can name the confusion accurately,  
I don't have to solve the entire problem before I speak.

That rule asks less of me than certainty.

It asks me to notice what doesn't line up,  
and say it while the conversation can still change.

Recommended ending — Story request — best for comments

I still don't know whether I'll name the confusion when the stakes are higher.  
When has naming the confusion helped you speak before you had the answer?

Alternate endings

Named reader — best for shares

I still don't know whether I'll name the confusion when the stakes are higher.  
This is for the person drafting a careful follow-up while the meeting is still happening.

Callback — best for cohesion

So, how certain do I need to be before I speak?  
When the stakes are higher, I still don't know whether I'll name the confusion.

Unapologized position

I still don't know whether I'll name the confusion when the stakes are higher.  
For now, naming it is enough reason for me to enter the conversation.

Light delivery notes

Open quietly. Pause after "Nobody blamed me" and the week-of-work line.  
Let the ending sound curious, not triumphant.

## 简体中文

我到底要多确定，才敢在会议里开口？

有几个月，我的答案是：

得百分之百确定。

所以开会时，我总是关着摄像头。

哪怕我已经准备过，  
也确实有些有用的东西想说，  
我还是会等。

这件事是从一次很早的会议开始的。

那天我讲一个想法，讲得太快了。

有人追问了一个问题，  
我答不上来。

没有人笑我。

也没有人怪我。

但我把那一刻当成了警告：

以后只有完全确定，才能开口。

从那以后，我换了一种参与方式。

开会的时候先不说，  
会后再发一份仔细写过的补充说明。

那样，我可以把每句话都检查清楚。

也不用在大家还在提问、改变方向时，  
暴露自己不知道的部分。

我还是有准备。

我还是能注意到一些有用的东西。

我也会把它们写下来。

看起来，我并没有停止参与。

我只是把参与的时间，推到了会后。

后来，一个项目卡住了。

两个人对同一个截止日期，  
一直有着不同的理解。

我在会议里听出了这个错位。

但我的第一反应，还是等。

等会议结束，  
再写一份完整、准确的说明。

最好连答案也一起准备好。

会议进行到一半，我没有继续等。

我说：“我觉得我们在解决两个不同的问题。”

当时，我并没有完整答案。

我只是请他们分别说清楚，  
自己用的是哪个日期，  
为什么用这个日期。

两个人把日期说出来以后，  
差别一下就摆在了所有人面前。

大家同意了下一步怎么走。

可会议结束后，我又开始担心。

我会不会只是说了一句大家都知道的话？

我听起来是不是太显而易见，  
根本算不上有帮助？

我没有解决整个问题，只是把混乱说清楚了。

后来，一个同事告诉我，  
那个问题帮他们省下了一周的工作。

我不会因为这一次，  
就说开口一定能改变一场会议。

我也不觉得，不确定本身就有价值。

有时候，一个没想完整的念头，  
确实只是一个没想完整的念头。

我现在也还是会等得太久。

只是我在练习一条小一点的规则。

如果我能准确说出大家混乱在哪里，  
就不用先解决整个问题，才有资格开口。

这条规则没有要求我变得更确定。

它只要求我在对话还能改变的时候，  
把那个对不上的地方说出来。

Recommended ending — Story request — best for comments

事情更重要时，我还不知道自己能不能把混乱说出来。  
你有没有一次，只因说清了混乱，就在没答案时开了口？

Alternate endings

Named reader — best for shares

事情更重要时，我还不知道自己能不能把混乱说出来。  
这段话送给那个会后说明写了一半，却还没发言的人。

Callback — best for cohesion

所以，我到底要多确定，才敢开口？  
事情更重要时，我还不知道自己能不能把混乱说出来。

Unapologized position

事情更重要时，我还不知道自己能不能把混乱说出来。  
但现在，只要能说清混乱，我就愿意先进入对话。

Light delivery notes

开头放慢。说完“没有人怪我”和“省下一周工作”后停一下。  
结尾留一点犹豫，不要讲成结论。
