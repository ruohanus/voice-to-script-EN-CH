For months I kept my camera off in meetings even when I had prepared something useful to say. I had started doing it after one early meeting where I explained an idea too quickly and someone asked a question I could not answer. Nobody laughed or blamed me, but I treated that moment as a warning that I should only speak when I was certain. The result was that I began sending careful follow-up notes after meetings instead of entering the conversation while it was still happening.

Then a project stalled because two people were making different assumptions about the same deadline. I had noticed the mismatch but planned to write a detailed note afterward. Halfway through the call, I said that I thought we were solving two different problems. I did not have the full answer. I asked each person what date they were using and why. Once the difference was visible, the group agreed on a next step. After the meeting, I worried I had sounded obvious rather than helpful. A teammate later told me the question had saved them a week of work.

I am not claiming that speaking up always changes a meeting or that uncertainty is good by itself. I still sometimes wait too long. But I am trying to practice a smaller rule: if I can name the confusion accurately, I do not have to solve the entire problem before I speak. I want the ending to leave open whether I can keep doing this when the stakes feel higher, while still inviting the viewer to notice one moment where naming confusion may be more useful than hiding until they are certain.
