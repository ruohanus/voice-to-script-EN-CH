## English

Is wanting different work enough reason to leave work you're demonstrably good at?

I worked in operations for nine years.
I became the person who caught problems before anyone else noticed them.
My team trusted me with difficult launches, and I liked being reliable.

Last winter, I arrived twenty minutes early for a Monday meeting.
I sat in my car with my laptop bag on the passenger seat.
I stayed there until three minutes before the meeting.

I wasn't afraid of the meeting.
I just couldn't make myself walk inside yet.

That evening, my partner asked why I kept volunteering for projects I complained about every night.
I said the team needed me.
That sounded responsible, and it was partly true.

Two weeks later, my manager offered me the promotion I'd been working toward.
"You've already been doing half the job."

I smiled and said I needed the weekend.
At home, I opened a blank document and listed the reasons to accept.

Salary.
Title.
Not disappointing people.

I couldn't write a fourth.

For six months, I'd also been taking an evening ceramics class.
I'm not saying ceramics should become my career.

What mattered was how I could spend two hours on one bowl without checking the time.
I hadn't felt that absorbed at work in years.

The contrast made something harder to ignore.

I kept calling my competence commitment.
It had become a way to avoid admitting I wanted different work.

On Sunday night, I declined the promotion.
I didn't resign.

I asked to work four days a week for three months.
I wanted room to explore other work without pretending I already knew the answer.

My manager agreed to a trial.

I may leave operations.
I may build something of my own.
I may discover I only needed a different pace.

I don't know yet.

I don't regret being good at operations.
I don't regret that people trusted me.

But I no longer want competence to make the decision for me.

Recommended ending — Open decision · keeps the decision open

I haven't left operations, and I still don't know where this trial leads.
I'm finding out whether wanting different work is enough reason to leave work I'm good at.
