## English

For nine years, I worked in operations.
I was good at catching problems before anyone else noticed them.
My team trusted me with difficult launches.
I liked being reliable.

Last winter, I arrived twenty minutes early for a Monday meeting.
I sat in my car outside the office.
My laptop bag was on the passenger seat.
I stayed there until three minutes before the meeting.

I wasn't afraid of the meeting.
I just couldn't make myself walk inside yet.

That evening, my partner asked why I kept volunteering for projects
I complained about every night.
I said the team needed me.
It sounded responsible.
It was also partly true.

Two weeks later, my manager offered me the promotion I'd been working toward.
The exact words I remember are,
"You've already been doing half the job."

I smiled and said I needed the weekend.

At home, I opened a blank document
and started listing reasons to accept.
Salary.
Title.
Not disappointing people.

I couldn't write a fourth.

For six months, I'd also been taking an evening ceramics class.
I'm not saying ceramics should become my career.
What mattered was simpler.
I could work on one bowl for two hours without checking the time.
I hadn't felt that absorbed at work in years.

That contrast was hard to ignore.
I had spent years proving I could do the work.
Somewhere along the way,
being good at it had started to sound like a reason to keep choosing it.

I kept calling my competence commitment.
But it had become a way to avoid admitting I wanted different work.

On Sunday night, I declined the promotion.

I didn't resign.
I asked to work four days a week for three months,
so I could explore other kinds of work
without pretending I already knew the answer.

My manager agreed to a trial.

I still don't know where this experiment leads.
I may leave operations.
I may build something of my own.
I may discover that I only needed a different pace.

Recommended ending — open decision · keeps the decision open

What I don't know is whether wanting different work
is enough reason to leave work I am demonstrably good at.

我在运营岗位做了九年。

我不是害怕那场会。
我只是还不想走进去。

那天晚上，伴侣问我，
为什么每天回家都在抱怨，
却还是不断主动接下那些项目。
我说，团队需要我。
这个回答听起来很负责，也有一部分是真的。

两周后，经理给了我一直在争取的晋升机会。
我清楚记得经理说：
“这份工作，你已经做了一半了。”

我笑了笑，说周末后再答复。

回到家，我打开一份空白文档，
开始列出接受晋升的理由。
薪水。
头衔。
不让别人失望。

第四条，我写不出来。

那时，我已经上了六个月的晚间陶艺课。
我不是说陶艺就该变成我的职业。
真正让我在意的是，
我可以对着一个碗做两个小时，
一次都不看时间。
我已经很多年没有在工作里这么投入了。

我花了很多年证明自己能把工作做好。
不知不觉间，擅长这件事，
也成了我继续做下去的理由。

我一直把能力叫作责任感。
可它已经成了一个办法，
让我不用承认，我想做不一样的工作。

周日晚上，我拒绝了那次晋升。

我没有辞职。
我申请三个月内每周只工作四天，
用空出来的时间探索其他工作，
也不假装自己已经知道答案。

经理同意先试一试。

我还是不知道这次尝试会把我带到哪里。
也许我会离开运营。
也许我会做自己的东西。
也许我只是需要换一种节奏。

推荐结尾 — 开放选择 · 保留尚未做完的决定
