[Quiet, reflective]

When is being responsive actually useful?

And when is it just a very convincing way to avoid the work?

I had to ask myself that after spending six weeks avoiding a proposal.

Not six weeks working on it. Six weeks avoiding it.

During that time, I still replied to clients. A message would come in, I would answer, and I would get this tiny feeling of importance.

Someone needed me.

I was available.

I was doing my job.

At least, that was what it felt like.

[Small pause]

But the proposal did not move.

That was the uncomfortable part. I could answer a message almost immediately, then keep putting off the work that actually required something from me.

Replying was clear. A client asked for something, and I responded.

The proposal was uncertain. I had to make something without knowing exactly how it would turn out. There was no immediate reply to reassure me. No little signal that I was useful. There was only the work.

So I cancelled two routine calls.

I did not stop replying to clients. I did not vanish or decide that communication no longer mattered. I just removed two things from my schedule that felt responsible but were not helping me finish.

Then I completed the proposal in one afternoon.

[Pause]

That afternoon changed how I saw the previous six weeks.

Being needed had become a hiding place.

It was a particularly comfortable hiding place because it looked like good client service. I could stay busy, stay responsive, and keep receiving proof that people valued me, all while avoiding the uncertain thing I needed to make.

I think this is easy to confuse when you freelance.

Responsiveness is visible. A client can see that you answered quickly.

Useful work is often much quieter. Nobody sends a notification asking you to think carefully. The unfinished proposal cannot follow up. The idea you have not developed cannot put time on your calendar.

So the messages win, unless you decide otherwise.

[Lean in slightly]

I still reply to clients.

I still care about being reliable.

But now I protect a two-hour block before I open my messages.

Those two hours belong to the work that cannot demand my attention. The proposal. The concept. The uncertain thing that needs enough silence for me to find out what it might become.

Opening the inbox first would still give me an immediate job to do. Protecting the block means I have to begin without that reassurance.

That is the distinction I was missing.

Being responsive can be useful. But the feeling of being needed is not proof that I am doing the most useful thing.

[Slow down]

So if you are answering everyone and still avoiding the work you say matters, ask yourself what your responsiveness is doing for you.

Is it moving the work forward?

Or is it giving you a safer feeling of importance while the uncertain work waits?

[Pause]

I do not want to be less responsible to my clients.

I want to remember that responsibility includes making the thing they cannot ask for yet.

Sometimes that means replying.

And sometimes it means leaving the messages closed for two hours and finally beginning.
